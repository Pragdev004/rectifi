# SIPs and mutual funds

A mutual fund pools money from many people and a fund manager invests it in shares, bonds or both. A SIP (systematic investment plan) is a way of putting a fixed amount into a fund on a fixed date each month.

## How a SIP works
Each month the same amount buys **units** of the fund at that day's price, called the NAV. When the price is low you get more units, and when it is high you get fewer. Over time this averages your buying price. It also builds a habit.

## What to know first
- Returns are **not guaranteed**. Equity funds can fall, sometimes sharply, and you should stay invested for several years.
- Do not invest money you may need within a few years. Build your emergency fund first.
- The **expense ratio** is the yearly fee the fund charges. Lower is better when funds are otherwise similar. Direct plans have lower fees than regular plans.
- Past returns do not predict future returns.

## Types in plain words
Equity funds hold shares (higher risk, higher possible growth). Debt funds hold bonds (usually steadier). Index funds copy a market index at low cost. Liquid funds hold very short-term bonds and suit parking spare cash.

## Staying safe
Invest only through SEBI-registered platforms and the fund house's official sites. Be careful with anyone promising fixed high returns or "guaranteed" profits. Those are warning signs of fraud. A SIP can be paused or stopped at any time.
