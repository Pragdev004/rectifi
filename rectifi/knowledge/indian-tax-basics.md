# Indian tax basics

This is a short, general guide. It has no figures on purpose, because limits and rates change. Check the official income tax website or a qualified tax professional for the current numbers.

## Who has to file
You must file an income tax return when your income is above the basic exemption limit for the year, and in some other cases. Even if you are not required to, filing can be useful to claim a refund of tax already cut (TDS) or to have a record of income.

## Words you will meet
- **PAN:** your permanent account number, needed for tax and many bank steps. Link it with your Aadhaar.
- **TDS:** tax deducted at source. The payer cuts tax before paying you, for example a bank on interest.
- **Form 16:** a salary certificate from an employer showing the tax cut.
- **Form 26AS and AIS:** your tax records. They show tax deducted and income reported against your PAN. Compare them with your own records.
- **Assessment year and financial year:** the financial year is when you earn. The assessment year is the year after, when you file.

## Old and new regime
There are two ways of calculating tax. One has lower rates with fewer deductions and one has higher rates with deductions, such as for certain savings and insurance. Compare both for your situation. The better one can change each year.

## Students and stipends
A scholarship, stipend or freelance income may or may not be taxable. Keep records of what you receive. Interest on a savings account can be taxable above a limit.

## Good habits
Keep payment receipts, file before the due date and never pay anyone who promises to "reduce tax" in unusual ways.
