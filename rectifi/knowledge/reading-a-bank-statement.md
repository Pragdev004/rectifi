# Reading a bank statement

A bank statement is the bank's own record of money going in and out of your account. Learn to read it and you can catch mistakes and fraud.

## The columns
- **Date** (sometimes also a value date): when the entry was posted.
- **Narration or description:** a short text about the payment. For UPI it often shows UPI, the payee's name or handle and a reference number.
- **Reference or cheque number:** the unique number for the transaction. For UPI it is a 12-digit reference. Keep it if you ever need to dispute a payment.
- **Debit or withdrawal:** money that left your account.
- **Credit or deposit:** money that came in.
- **Balance:** the running balance after that entry.

## Check the maths
The opening balance, plus all credits, minus all debits, should equal the closing balance. If it does not, a row is missing or wrong. Rectifi checks this when you import a statement and shows any gap.

## Things to look for
Charges you do not recognise, the same payment twice, small test payments from strangers and fees for services you never asked for. If something looks wrong, contact your bank soon and give the reference number.

## Tips
Download statements as a file from your bank's official site or app. A PDF statement is often protected with a password the bank gives you. Do not share a statement with people you do not trust: it shows your account number and your habits.
