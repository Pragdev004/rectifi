# Credit card basics

A credit card lets you spend the bank's money now and pay later. It is a loan with a deadline. Used well it is convenient and builds a credit history. Used badly it is expensive debt.

## How the bill works
- The **billing cycle** is about a month. Everything you spend in that cycle goes on one statement.
- The **due date** is when you must pay, usually a few weeks after the statement.
- The **interest-free period** only applies if you pay the **total amount due** in full by the due date.

## The minimum due trap
Paying only the minimum due keeps your account in good standing, but the rest of the balance starts charging interest, often at a high yearly rate, from the purchase date. Interest on interest builds quickly. If you cannot pay in full, pay as much as you can above the minimum.

## Other costs to watch
Late payment fees, cash advance charges (interest starts the same day), foreign currency fees, annual fees and GST on charges. Read the fee table before you apply.

## Credit score
Your credit score reflects how reliably you repay. Paying on time, keeping your balance well below your limit and not applying for many cards at once all help. A missed payment hurts it.

## Simple rules
Spend only what you could pay from your account today. Set up auto-pay for the full amount. Track card spending in Rectifi like any other account so it does not surprise you.
